How it works

Your student responds. You coach.

Most emergencies on campus start with a friend and a phone call. BackUp Pack gives both ends of that call a plan.

  1. Student checks the scene

    Makes sure it is safe and calls 911 for anything in Pouches 1 to 3.

  2. Student grabs the pouch

    Each pouch is numbered and labeled, with a step card inside that shows one action per line.

  3. Parent coaches by phone

    You stay calm, ask three simple questions, and keep your student on the line.

  4. Help arrives

    Your student tells EMS what was done and when.

For Pouches 1 to 3, a parent’s first move is always the same: make sure 911 has been called.

The training

Two courses for two different roles

Your student needs hands-on skills. You need a calm script. So each of you gets your own short course.

Student responder

Learn to respond

  • One sequence to remember: check, call 911, grab the pouch, stay
  • Practice scenarios built around helping a friend
  • What to say to EMS when they arrive
  • How to support a friend after a mental health or assault disclosure

Parent coach

Learn to coach

  • Your first move for life-threatening emergencies
  • Three questions to ask: breathing, responsive, heavy bleeding?
  • How to stay calm and keep your student on the line
  • How to listen after a hard call, without fixing or blaming

Training is in development. Everyone who reserves a kit gets first access.

Questions

What parents ask first

Is this a replacement for calling 911?

No. For anything in Pouches 1 to 3, the first step is calling 911. The kit and training help your student act in the first minutes before help arrives. They don’t replace emergency services or professional medical care.

Can a parent really help by phone?

Within limits. You can’t see the situation, so your role is to stay calm, make sure 911 has been called, and keep your student focused on the step card. The parent course teaches exactly where that role starts and stops.

Why are there two courses?

Your student and you do different jobs in an emergency. Separate courses keep each one short and focused.

Does the kit include prescription medication?

No. If your student has a prescription, such as an epinephrine auto-injector, they should carry it with them.

Will you share my information?

We’ll only use your email to tell you about your reservation and the training. We’ll never share your student’s activity with you or anyone else.
